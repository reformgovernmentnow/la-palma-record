01

Litigation: The Cases City Hall Can't Ignore

A police officer's discrimination complaints went undocumented and uninvestigated; a jury later awarded $8.4 million, and the City's appeal is pending. The City now faces two additional lawsuits involving alleged unpaid wages and a fatal police pursuit.

  • Dec. 2025: Jury awards $8.4 million in Byer v. City of La Palma; appeal pending
  • Mar. 2026: Wrongful-death lawsuit filed over a December 2024 police pursuit
  • Apr. 2026: Former employee files unpaid-wage lawsuit in Torres v. City of La Palma
  • Aug. 2026: Council has not committed to an independent investigation

02

Term Limits: Who Wrote, Paid for, and Benefited from Measure W?

In 2019, the Council considered extending term limits but tabled the proposal after strong resident opposition. Five years later, a similar proposal returned as Measure W — supported by taxpayer-funded outreach and a private campaign substantially financed by sitting council members.

  • 2019: Term-limit extension tabled after 11 residents spoke in opposition
  • May 2024: City consultant drafted ballot language before the Council's public vote
  • June 2024: Measure W excluded prior service from the new 12-year limit
  • Nov. 2024: Measure W passed with 58%; ballot question did not disclose the prior-service reset
  • Nov. 2024: Yes on W substantially financed by three sitting council members

03

Pension Debt: What Happened to the Nine-Year Payoff

Voters approved a permanent 1% sales tax in 2016 after being told the City's unfunded pension liability would be paid off in nine years and save $20 million. Nine years later, the liability was higher — and CalPERS still projects payments into roughly 2040–41.

  • 2016: Unfunded Liability $18.2 million
  • 2025: Unfunded Liability $23.6 million
  • Promise: Paid off in nine years by FY 2025-26 and save $20 million
  • Reality: Current CalPERS projection FY 2040–41, no savings identified
  • Aug. 2025: Nitesh Patel claims at a council meeting the liability had “gone down”

04

Transparency: Why Couldn't Residents Watch Council Meetings Online

Regular Council meetings were cut from twice monthly to once monthly during COVID and never restored. From 2023 through 2025, the Council majority repeatedly declined to implement video livestreaming. In 2026, state law required remote access.

  • 2020: Regular meetings reduced from twice monthly to once monthly — never restored
  • 2023: La Palma is one of only two Orange County cities without livestreaming, gets a 'D' grade for transparency
  • 2023–25: Council majority repeatedly declined proposals to add video
  • Oct. 2025: SB 707 required remote access to public meetings by July 2026
  • Aug. 2026: City's planned livestream launch delayed until October

05

Leadership: A Decade of Turnover at City Hall

Over the past decade, La Palma has experienced repeated transitions among its City Managers, Police Chiefs, and City Attorneys — including four police chiefs in about 26 months and several major appointments without a documented competitive search.

  • City Managers: Three over the decade; one resignation received no standalone City announcement
  • Police Chiefs: Four different chiefs in roughly 26 months
  • City Attorney: Firm transition with no competitive review identified in the public record
  • 2025: Former Councilmember Peter Kim selected as City Manager from 87 applicants
  • Appointments: Several key leadership transitions relied on internal or existing relationships rather than open competition
