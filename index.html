This page presents a fact-based record built from public records, sworn filings, and the City's own documents and records. Five threads run through the last decade of La Palma governance. Each is summarized below, with the full sourced account and timeline one click away.

01

Term Limits: From 1996 to Measure W

In 2019, the council proposed extending term limits and tabled it after residents packed a meeting to oppose it. Five years later, essentially the same proposal returned as Measure W — this time backed by a taxpayer-funded outreach contract and a privately funded campaign run by the officials it benefited.

  • Aug.–Sept. 2019: proposal advanced 3-0, then tabled 5-0 after 11 residents spoke against it
  • May 2024: City paid a consultant to draft ballot language before any public vote
  • Measure W was written so prior service didn't count toward the new limit — sitting members could serve up to 20 consecutive years without a break
  • Nov. 2024: Measure W passes 58% — ballot language never disclosed the incumbent term reset
  • Yes on W funded substantially by three sitting council members and family

02

Measure JJ and the Pension Debt

Voters approved a permanent 1% sales tax in 2016 on a promise the pension debt would be paid off in nine years. It rose nearly 30% instead — and in 2025, a sitting official told the council the opposite from the dais.

  • 2016: UAL was $18.2M; promised payoff in 9 years
  • 2025: UAL had grown to $23.6M — a 29.8% increase
  • Aug. 2025: council member states liability had "gone down"; audited records say otherwise
  • CalPERS' own June 2024 valuation: $25.3M combined UAL, not projected to be paid off until roughly 2040-41 — the campaign promised 9 years

03

Leadership Transitions

City Manager, Police Chief, and City Attorney all changed hands across the decade — usually through internal promotion, and often without the due diligence of an external search to identify the best and most qualified talent available.

  • Two city managers in, one departure never publicly announced to residents
  • Four police chiefs in roughly 26 months
  • City Attorney's firm changed with no competitive review on record
  • 2025: former councilmember appointed City Manager over 86 other applicants

04

Litigation

A police officer's discrimination complaints went undocumented and uninvestigated — a jury later awarded $8.4 million (the City's appeal is pending). Four months on, a second former employee sued over unpaid wages under nearly identical institutional silence. Separately, the City also faces a pending wrongful death suit over a December 2024 police pursuit.

  • Dec. 2025: $8.4M verdict in Byer v. City of La Palma; City appeals — appeal still pending as of August 2026
  • Apr. 2026: second lawsuit filed — Torres v. City of La Palma
  • Mar. 2026: $30M wrongful death claim in Casey v. City of La Palma et al. over a December 2024 police pursuit — pending, no trial date set
  • Council has not committed to an independent investigation

05

Public Meeting Access

Meetings were cut in half under COVID and never restored. Video livestreaming was refused three years running — while the City found $80,000 for other technology — until a 2026 state law removed the choice. Even then, the City announced a delay to its own promised start date one day before the meeting where it was due to begin.

  • 2020: regular meetings cut from twice-monthly to once monthly, never restored
  • 2023–2024: only 1 of 2 OC cities without livestreaming; lowest accessibility grade
  • 2026: streaming ordered under state law (SB 707) — then delayed to October, announced only a few days before the August 4, 2026 meeting
