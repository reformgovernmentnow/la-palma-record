This page summarizes a fact-based record built entirely from public records, sworn filings, and the City's own documents. Five threads run through the last decade of La Palma governance — each is summarized below, with the full sourced account one click away.

01

Term Limits: From 1996 to Measure W

In 2019, the council proposed extending term limits and tabled it after residents packed a meeting to oppose it. Five years later, essentially the same proposal returned as Measure W — this time backed by a taxpayer-funded outreach contract and a privately funded campaign run by the officials it benefited.

  • Aug.–Sept. 2019: proposal advanced 3-0, then tabled 5-0 after 11 residents spoke against it
  • May 2024: City paid a consultant to draft ballot language before any public vote
  • Nov. 2024: Measure W passes 58% — ballot language never disclosed the incumbent term reset
  • Yes on W funded substantially by three sitting council members and family

02

Measure JJ and the Pension Record

Voters approved a permanent 1% sales tax in 2016 on a promise the pension debt would be paid off in nine years. It rose nearly 30% instead — and in 2025, a sitting official told the council the opposite from the dais.

  • 2016: UAL was $18.2M; promised payoff in 9 years
  • 2025: UAL had grown to $23.6M — a 29.8% increase
  • Aug. 2025: council member states liability had "gone down"; audited records say otherwise

03

Leadership Transitions

City Manager, Police Chief, and City Attorney all changed hands repeatedly across the decade — usually through internal promotion, with no external search and limited public explanation each time.

  • Two city managers in, one departure never publicly announced to residents
  • Four police chiefs in roughly 26 months
  • City Attorney's firm changed with no competitive review on record
  • 2025: former councilmember appointed City Manager over 86 other applicants

04

Employment Litigation

A police officer's discrimination complaints went undocumented and uninvestigated — a jury later awarded $8.4 million. Four months on, a second former employee sued over unpaid wages under nearly identical institutional silence.

  • Dec. 2025: $8.4M verdict in Byer v. City of La Palma; City appeals
  • Apr. 2026: second lawsuit filed — Torres v. City of La Palma
  • Council has not committed to an independent investigation
Read the full Litigation section
05

Public Meeting Access

Meetings were cut in half under COVID and never restored. Video livestreaming was refused three years running — while the City found $80,000 for other technology — until a 2026 state law removed the choice.

  • 2020: regular meetings cut from twice-monthly to once monthly, never restored
  • 2023–2024: only 1 of 2 OC cities without livestreaming; lowest accessibility grade
  • 2026: streaming finally implemented — only once state law (SB 707) required it

What Voters Can Do

Ask every District 2 and District 4 candidate these questions, on the record. A candidate's answers — and any refusal to answer — will tell you what you need to know.

01Will you vote to restore twice-monthly regular council meetings? (This requires only a council resolution — three votes could do it at any meeting.)

02Will you vote to commission a truly independent, external investigation of the police department's complaint-handling practices, with authority to review records and interview personnel?

03Will you direct staff to present a full public accounting of every Measure JJ promise made in 2016 against actual results — pension payoff, the $20 million in savings, reserves, the stabilization fund, and the Utility Users Tax?

04Will you vote to repeal the two-member consent requirement for placing items on the council agenda?

05Will you support posting all campaign finance filings on the city website, as neighboring cities already do?

06Did you support or oppose Measure W, and did you or your campaign receive support from any sitting council member or their family?

07Will you commit to never spending General Fund money on consultants to draft or promote ballot measures that extend council members' own tenure — and to disclosing, in any city-funded material about a future measure, every provision that benefits sitting incumbents?

08Before approving any future promotion of a near-retirement employee into a higher-paid position, will you require a public staff report disclosing the projected lifetime pension cost of that promotion?

09Do you believe the City Manager should be an independent professional administrator rather than a former council colleague — and will you support recruiting professional city management for future vacancies?

These questions are drawn directly from documented gaps in the public record described above. They are offered so voters can ask their own questions of candidates — not as an implied answer.
