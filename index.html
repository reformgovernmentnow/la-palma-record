Five Things La Palma Residents Should Know

A fact-based look at five major issues shaping La Palma City Hall, drawn from public records, sworn filings, and the City's own documents. Each topic is summarized below, with the full sourced record one click away.

01

The Cases City Hall Can’t Ignore

A police officer's discrimination complaints went undocumented and uninvestigated — a jury later awarded $8.4 million (the City's appeal is pending). Four months after the verdict, a second former employee sued over unpaid wages under nearly identical institutional silence. Separately, the City also faces a pending wrongful death suit over a December 2024 police pursuit.

  • Dec. 2025: $8.4M verdict in Byer v. City of La Palma; City appeals — appeal still pending as of August 2026
  • Apr. 2026: second lawsuit filed over unpaid wages — Torres v. City of La Palma
  • Mar. 2026: $30M wrongful death claim in Casey v. City of La Palma et al. over a December 2024 police pursuit — pending, no trial date set
  • As of Aug. 2026: Council has not committed to an independent investigation

02

Term Limits: Who Wrote, Paid for, and Benefited from Measure W?

In 2019, the council proposed extending term limits and tabled it after residents packed a meeting to oppose it. Five years later, essentially the same proposal returned as Measure W — this time backed by a taxpayer-funded outreach contract and a privately funded campaign run by the officials it benefited.

  • Aug.–Sept. 2019: proposal advanced 3-0, then tabled 5-0 after 11 residents spoke against it
  • May 2024: City paid a consultant to draft ballot language before any public vote
  • June 2024: Measure W was written so prior service didn't count toward the new limit — sitting members could serve up to 20 consecutive years without a break
  • Nov. 2024: Measure W passes 58% — ballot language never disclosed the incumbent term reset
  • Oct.–Nov. 2024: Yes on W funded substantially by three sitting council members and family

03

What Happened to the Measure JJ Pension Payoff Promise?

Voters approved a permanent 1% sales tax in 2016 on a promise the pension debt would be paid off in nine years. It rose nearly 30% instead — and in 2025, Nitesh Patel told the council the opposite from the dais.

  • 2016: the pension debt (UAL) was $18.2M; promised payoff in 9 years
  • 2025: UAL had grown to $23.6M — a 29.8% increase
  • Aug. 2025: Patel states liability had "gone down"; audited records say otherwise
  • CalPERS' own June 2024 valuation: $25.3M combined UAL, not projected to be paid off until roughly 2040-41 — the campaign promised 9 years

04

Why Couldn't Residents Watch Council Meetings?

Meetings were cut in half under COVID and never restored. Video livestreaming was refused three years running — while the City found $80,000 for other technology — until a 2026 state law removed the choice. Even then, the City announced a delay to its own promised start date one day before the meeting where it was due to begin.

  • 2020: regular meetings cut from twice-monthly to once monthly, never restored
  • 2023: one of only 2 OC cities without livestreaming — the lowest accessibility grade in the county
  • 2023–2026: council majority repeatedly rejected public requests and council member motions to implement video livestreaming
  • 2026: streaming ordered under state law (SB 707), then pushed to October — the delay announced just days before the August 4, 2026 meeting it had promised to livestream

05

What's Happened to City Leadership?

City Manager, Police Chief, and City Attorney all changed hands across the decade — usually through internal promotion, and often without the due diligence of an external search to identify the best and most qualified talent available.

  • Three city managers over the decade — one resignation never got a standalone announcement from the City
  • Four police chiefs in roughly 26 months
  • City Attorney's firm changed with no competitive review on record
  • 2025: Council appoints a former councilmember as City Manager over 86 other applicants
