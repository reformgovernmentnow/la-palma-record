Public Meeting Access and Transparency

How La Palma cut public meetings in half in 2020, refused video livestreaming for three years, and complied only once state law required it in 2026.

Covers the 2020 meeting-frequency cut, three years in which the council majority repeatedly refused residents' requests to livestream video, the agenda-gatekeeping rule that followed, and the 2026 compliance that came only after the State of California — not the council — removed the choice.

June 2, 2020 — Citing the COVID-19 emergency, the City Council adopts Resolution 2020-29, cutting regular council meetings in half — from the twice-monthly schedule the city had maintained throughout its history to once per month. The City's own later announcement admits the change was made “in an effort to conduct City business during the COVID-19 pandemic” and simply “has become current practice.” Unlike virtually every other California city that consolidated meetings during COVID, La Palma never restored its normal schedule. The result: the public's regular opportunities to address its government on the record were permanently cut from roughly 24 per year to 12 — and in practice fewer, as the single July meeting is routinely cancelled.

February 7, 2023 — Councilmember Janet Keo Conklin urges the council to consider video livestreaming of public meetings. Councilmembers Nitesh Patel, Mark Waldman, and Debbie Baker oppose it. The council rejects even a proposal by Councilmember Goodman to have staff merely research streaming options without committing any city funds. Patel acknowledges the city has elderly residents, but says he thinks “there is enough information out there that gets out to them.”

2023–2024 — A Voice of OC / Chapman University investigation grades all 34 Orange County cities on public meeting accessibility. Every city receives an A or B — except La Palma and Rancho Santa Margarita, the only two cities in the county refusing to livestream video. Each receives a D. A First Amendment Coalition legal fellow says the lack of virtual access “keeps residents in the dark.” Then-Councilmember Nitesh Patel told Voice of OC his concerns centered on cost, infrastructure, and how the city would implement it, adding that other cities that already streamed video were larger than La Palma.

November 5, 2024 — The same council approves roughly $80,000 in IT upgrades for City Hall — Microsoft Office, a new phone system — while continuing to claim the city cannot afford video streaming. Cities with comparable budgets (Stanton, Los Alamitos) had been streaming for years. Waldman's on-record explanation for the contradiction: “It's different stuff.”

December 2024 – January 2025 — At the council's December 10 meeting, Councilmember Janet Keo Conklin proposes adding video directly — “I propose we now look into [adding] audio and visual,” she says — pointing to Census data showing roughly one in five La Palma residents are of retirement age, and telling Voice of OC afterward that she hoped to bring a proposal forward for greater transparency and accountability. No colleague seconds a further push, and the council instead adopts, 4-1, a “Code of Conduct” requiring two council members' consent to place any item on the public agenda — a rule that structurally silences the lone member who kept raising transparency. The pattern repeats at the January 14 meeting, where Keo Conklin's motion to direct staff to add video dies again for lack of a second. “So they don't want accountability or transparency. What is wrong with video streaming?” she says afterward. Mayor Waldman had separately told Voice of OC that few people listen to the audio stream anyway. When she presses the question “what are we hiding?” at the dais, the mayor declares a five-minute recess.

May 2025 — Voice of OC revisits the story, confirming Keo Conklin's January 14 motion had died for lack of a second from any other member. Samuel Stone, a public administration professor at Fullerton College, offers outside context on the pattern: “Just doing the minimum is not always what everybody wants or expects.”

April–July 2026 — Only now — because SB 707, the state's rewrite of the Brown Act effective July 1, 2026, mandates two-way remote public participation — does the council unanimously direct staff to implement video, after Voice of OC had reported that every other Orange County city — all 32 of the others — already offered video livestreaming while La Palma provided audio only. The staff report accompanying the item tells the council that state lawmakers are expected to eventually require video statewide regardless of SB 707. The city contracts with Western Audio Visual — a vendor and target date Voice of OC later confirmed — projecting the system will be running by August 2026. The council did not act on its own: after three years in which the majority repeatedly refused residents' requests to add video, the change came only because the State of California removed the choice, not because the council chose transparency on its own. Mayor Patel — who as a councilmember opposed or declined to support the idea in 2023, 2024, and 2025 — now calls the change he resisted for three years “a great thing to do.”

August 3, 2026 — One day before the council's regular August 4 meeting, the City posts on its official Facebook page that the video livestreaming system it says the council approved on June 2, 2026 will not be ready as promised. Citing “equipment delays” — with no further detail offered — the post states the City now anticipates video streaming will not begin until the October 6, 2026 meeting, pushing back by at least two months the August 2026 target reported when the council directed staff to pursue the system in April. The disclosure arrives not as a proactive announcement timed for advance public notice, but as a same-week social media post the day before the very meeting residents might otherwise have expected to be the first livestreamed session. It is the latest instance of a now-familiar pattern in this record: a public-access commitment whose setbacks surface only at the last possible moment.
